Worked solution

Half precision & G/R/S rules

A normal half-precision value is (−1)^s × 1.f × 2^(E − 15). Normal unbiased exponents run from −14 to +15. Round each input to its stored half value before aligning and adding.

Keep 11 significant bits (the implicit leading bit plus 10 fraction bits). G is the next bit, R is the bit after G, and S is the OR of all remaining discarded bits.

Default classroom rule: increment only when G = 1 and R or S is 1. When G = 1, R = 0, S = 0, keep the retained bits, even if the retained last bit is 1. This rounds to nearest with exact ties toward zero.

Optional IEEE nearest-even rule: exact ties keep an even retained last bit (0), or increment an odd retained last bit (1). Both modes use the same rule for storing operands and rounding the final result.

A significand carry may increase the exponent. An overflowing result uses exponent 11111 and fraction 0000000000; the sign selects +INF or −INF. The underflow-to-zero policy is an exercise convention: standard IEEE half precision also supports subnormals.

The COD example uses A = 26.125 and B = 0.4150390625. Decimal numbers are displayed in full, without scientific notation.
