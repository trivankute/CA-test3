Worked solution

Half precision & G/R/S rules

A normal half-precision value is (−1)^s × 1.f × 2^(E − 15). Normal unbiased exponents run from −14 to +15. Round each input to its stored half value before multiplying. The product sign is the XOR of the operand signs. Multiply the significands and add the unbiased exponents; normalize the product, then round.

Keep 11 significant bits (the implicit leading bit plus 10 fraction bits). G is the next bit, R is the bit after G, and S is the OR of all remaining discarded bits. Increment the retained significand when G = 1 and (R = 1 or S = 1 or the retained last bit is 1). This implements round to nearest, ties to even. When G = 1 and R = S = 0, keep an even retained last bit (0), or increment an odd retained last bit (1) to make it even. The rule applies equally to positive and negative values.

A significand carry may increase the exponent. An overflowing result uses exponent 11111 and fraction 0000000000; the sign selects +INF or −INF. The underflow-to-zero policy is an exercise convention: standard IEEE half precision also supports subnormals.

The example uses A = 26.125 and B = 0.4150390625. Decimal numbers are displayed in full, without scientific notation.
